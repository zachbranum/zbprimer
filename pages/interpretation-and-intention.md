# Interpretation and intention

<p id="intention-heading">The fourth intention focuses on what other communication theories overlook. For example, when submitting their resume, applicants will intentionally encode their skills, experiences, and qualifications in their resume expecting a human to interpret it, but in reality today when it’s an AI agent being the first to see and make a decision on the resume it can be interpreted differently. The algorithm’s only intention is to scan the resumes and to determine if the applicant qualifies or not. This creates a new form of communication power where the algorithm can influence the outcome before a human can make a decision.

While there may be bias and a lot of people say there is bias with AI making decisions on people in the hiring process before seen by a human it is difficult to actually blame AI for it. In Why the algorithmic recruiter discriminates: The causal challenges of data-driven discrimination Christine Carter says, “proof of this is often unavailable due to the statistical complexities and operational opacities of machine learning.” That is what makes it so tough to solve this problem, because there is no proof that there even is a problem at all. Companies would rather use AI to speed up the process and make it easier on them even if it is unfair for the people applying.
