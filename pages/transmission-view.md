# The transmission view and its limits

<p id="transmission-heading">This page explores how the Shannon-Weaver model helps identify breakdowns in message transmission, especially where clarity, channel noise, and feedback loops matter. It is especially useful for diagnosing technical systems and process communication.</p>

<p>At the same time, humanist critiques remind us that communication is not only about successful delivery. Relationships, context, ethics, and lived interpretation shape whether a message is meaningful or actionable.</p>

<p>The communication transmission view of Shannon and Weaver where there is a sender and receiver in every type of interaction and communication is still present in this phenomenon. However, when AI enters the channel it can affect the message. In this example the applicant sends in their resume through an online portal/website for the job they are interested in. The resume then gets filed in their system and it is up to the company’s AI agent to determine if the resume matches the job description well enough to move on. The resumes the AI agent approves of get sent to a hiring manager who then looks over a much smaller stack of resumes than were actually submitted. In this model noise is represented by different resume formats, wording, and other stylings AI may not know how to interpret. There is a big difference between how humans may critique a resume or job application and how a certain model of AI can interpret it. This shows the limitations of the model because it treats communication as something that can be measured and transferred, while the actual hiring process involves subjective meaning and the consequences of how a person is interpreted by AI can be the difference in someone getting hired or not. 

On the other hand, using AI in this communication system can create a noiseless transmission. C. E. Shannon’s essay, A Mathematical Theory of Communication, goes into tremendous detail about the general schematic communication system. There is a section in this essay about discrete noiseless systems. In this section Shannon states, “in a noiseless telegraph channel one could save about 50% in time by proper encoding of the messages” (Shannon). AI does have the ability to cut out a lot of noise that could disrupt a human making AI in the channel more efficient and consistent. This theory was made in 1948 but is still relevant today where technology has tremendously changed.</p>
